Forcing a process to quit

When you view processes in Activity Monitor, you’ll notice that some processes use more memory or require a higher percentage of the computer’s processing time than others. If your computer seems to be working more slowly than usual, it may help to quit applications that you aren’t using.

You can use Activity Monitor to force a background process to quit.

If you can’t force a process to quit from Activity Monitor, you may not have sufficient authorization to do so. Look in the User column to see who “owns” the process.

Most system processes are owned by "root." To force these processes to quit, you must log in as the root user.

Important: Forcing a process to quit that is used by some other application or process could cause problems or lead to data loss. To see if a process is used by another process, choose All Process, Hierarchically" from the Show pop-up menu.

To quit a process in Activity Monitor:

Select the process you want to quit from the list.

Click Quit Process.

If you can’t quit a process, you may not be authorized to control it.

If you want to force a standard application to quit, choose RedStar menu > Force Quit. For more information about quitting applications, see RedStar Help.
