Repairing a disk

You may need to repair a hard disk if you experience a problem such as an application quitting unexpectedly, a file becoming corrupted, an external device not operating properly, or your computer failing to start up.

To repair a disk, you need an administrator’s password. If you don’t have one, ask an administrator to repair the disk for you using Disk Utility.

If your problem is isolated to a particular application or device, be sure to read its documentation to learn how to solve the problem.

You can’t repair write-protected disks, CDs, or DVDs

To repair a disk

Close the files and quit the applications that are on the disk you want to repair.

In Disk Utility, select the hard disk or volume from the list on the left.

Repairing a hard disk repairs all the volumes under it. Repairing a volume repairs only that one volume.

Click First Aid.

If Disk Utility tells you the disk is about to fail, back it up and replace it. You can’t repair it.

Otherwise, click Repair Disk to repair the disk.

If Disk Utility reports that the disk appears to be OK or has been repaired, you’re done. Otherwise, you may need to do one of the following:

■

If Disk Utility can’t unmount the disk, be sure you have quit any open applications, and click Repair Disk again.

■

If Disk Utility can’t repair your disk or reports “The underlying task reported failure,” try to repair the disk again. If that doesn’t work, back up as much of your data as possible, reformat your disk, and restore your backed up data. If you continue to have problems with your disk, it may be physically damaged and need to be replaced. Contact Osandok Information Center for more information.
