  Using Gigabit Ethernet

Some Macintosh computers support Gigabit Ethernet, which provides a data rate of 1 billion bits per second and requires a special Gigabit Ethernet cable.

If your computer and the device you are connecting to support Gigabit Ethernet, but the Ethernet cable you are using does not, you may need to adjust the Ethernet speed, or you can purchase a cable that supports Gigabit Ethernet.

To adjust the Ethernet speed of your computer:

Choose RedStar menu > System Preferences, and then click Network.

Select your Ethernet port configuration (named Ethernet unless you created another) from the Services list.

Click Advanced, and then click Ethernet.

Choose Manually from the Configure pop-up menu, and then choose a speed from the Speed pop-up menu.

You can also adjust duplex and the maximum transmission unit (MTU) settings using the pop-up menus. Check with your network administrator for the settings specific to the network.

These advanced settings affect the way your computer interacts with your network. Unless your network administrator has given you specific settings, use the automatic settings. Entering the wrong settings can affect network performance.
