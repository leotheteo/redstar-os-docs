  Keeping your computer information safe

If your computer is on a network or other people have access to it, you can do several things to make it more secure.

Require users to log in
Log out when you leave your computer. This forces other users to log in if they want to use the computer and prevents any tampering in your account.
■

Use good, secure passwords
Use secure passwords that aren't simplistic and that can't be easily guessed.
■
Creating secure passwords
Secure your computer when it's idle
Require a password to access your computer after it wakes from sleep.
■

■

Reduce the use of administrator accounts and the root user
Create another user that is not an administrator, and use that account when you don't need administrator privileges. Limit the number of users with administrator privileges.
If you have enabled the root user and no longer need it, you should disable the root user.

■

Log out when you are done using a secure account
Log out when you are finished working with a secure account on a computer, website, terminal, or other account.
Some secure accounts and websites will not log you out automatically if you quit or leave without logging out. This can leave your information vulnerable.
In some cases, deactivating a feature, such as remote login, may not log out users who are currently logged in. You should be sure that all users are logged out when you remove access.
